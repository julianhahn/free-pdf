# Vision

Julian's decision, 2026-08-13. Every design round is measured against this page. If a picture
disagrees with it, the picture is wrong.

## What the app is

FreePDF turns sheets of paper into one PDF, with the camera. Photograph the pages, the app
cleans each photo up, then it makes the PDF. Everything stays on the phone: no account, no
sign-in, no server, no settings screen. Nothing leaves until the user taps Share.

It is a tool, not an opinion. It hands the controls over instead of deciding: where the paper
edges are, how straight, how bright, how sharp. Every control opens on the value the engine
suggested, and the user may just accept it.

## Who holds it

Someone standing over a table with a stack of paper in one hand and the phone in the other.
One-handed, often. Sometimes in bad light - a kitchen at night, an office corridor, a cellar.
They want the scan done and the app gone.

## The direction: paper and archive

A light ground, serif titles, fine hairlines, a lot of quiet. The app should feel like a
well-kept folder of documents. Not like a phone app, and not like a piece of measuring
equipment.

The reason: the app's whole subject is paper. A style borrowed from the paper it scans is the
one style that explains itself without a word.

## Identical everywhere except mechanics

Colours, type, spacing and shapes are the **same on every client**. Only gestures and control
mechanics follow the platform: a swipe is the platform's swipe, a switch behaves like the
platform's switch - but it must not **look** like the platform's switch.

The reason, and this one has been standing since the start: one style is defined once and
rebuilt natively per client, because a platform-native look is by definition not recognizable
across platforms. Two clients that each look like their own operating system are not one
product.

## The tension to solve, not to ignore

A paper aesthetic has to survive a camera viewfinder, a shutter and sliders. Paper is light,
still and quiet; a viewfinder is dark and moving, a shutter is pressed with a thumb without
looking, a slider is dragged. A hairline on a dark preview is not a control, it is a
decoration.

Do not answer this by leaving the style. Answer it inside the style: weight, size, contrast and
a solid shape where a stroke is too thin. Three screens decide it, and a round is judged on
them:

- **the camera** - the viewfinder frame, the counter and the shutter on a dark, moving picture
- **the adjust controls** - sliders and switches, read and dragged at arm's length
- **the destructive actions** - delete scan, delete page, delete photos, in a palette with no red

## What would make the design wrong

A reviewer can fail a round on any one of these:

- it looks like a stock platform app - system font, system switch, system list
- the shutter needs a second glance to find
- it shows no page images anywhere; the app is full of pictures and the design has none
- it is text-heavy where the content is pictures
- a control whose state cannot be seen at arm's length
