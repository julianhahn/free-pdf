Flow 6 — adjusting a page

S24 to S32. Suggest-then-apply: every control opens on the value the app suggested, which is what Slider’s tick is for, and the user moves it or leaves it. There is no live preview anywhere — the picture is the page as it stands now. S23 is the delivered AdjustScreen and is reviewed on the foundation sheet; the strip it draws inline becomes ToolStrip, and the corner dragging it lacks becomes PageHandles.

S26 — spec

Built from two Slider and one Switch, in this order: Black point (how dark the darkest part becomes), White point (how bright the paper becomes), then the switch. Both sliders open where the app suggested, both keep their tick, one “Back to the suggestion” resets the group.

Why the min and max labels are sentences the numbers mean nothing on their own and there is no preview to check them against, so each end says what happens there. This is the one place the labels are long — they wrap to two lines and the layout is not pinned, which is also what German needs.

Scrolling at large text sizes this group is taller than the screen: the page shrinks first (down to a readable minimum), then the content scrolls under a fixed top bar and footer. Nothing is truncated.

VoiceOver “Black point, 12 per cent. The app suggests 12 per cent.” · “White point, 92 per cent…” · switch: “Even out the lighting. Evens out a page that is brighter on one side.” The switch label and its sub-line are not in the copy tables — confirm or replace.

S29 — spec

Built from one IconButton (outlined, rotate-cw), one quarter turn clockwise per tap. Shown after one tap: the page is landscape in its frame, centred in the same block the portrait page used, so nothing else on the screen moves.

No second control there is no anticlockwise button and no 180° button — four taps come back to the start, and one control cannot be mistaken for two. If a counter-clockwise turn is wanted, that is a copy and layout decision, not a component one; say so and it becomes a second IconButton.

VoiceOver “Turn the page a quarter turn clockwise.” After each tap the page’s own label is re-announced: “Page 3, turned a quarter turn clockwise.”

S31

Applying to all pages

A full-screen takeover. Every page is rewritten from its own photo, one at a time.
Page 12 of 40

S31 — spec

Order no app bar and no back arrow — that is the takeover · the block centred: PageCounter “Page 12 of 40”, space-4, ProgressLine with the line, the bar and the note “Keep the app open.”

How it is distinct from the drain (S13) without a colour three ways, all structural: the drain keeps the app bar and its back arrow, this has neither, so leaving is visibly not on offer; the drain has one line, this has two — a PageCounter above the line, which the drain never shows; and the notes say opposite things in the same position. Nothing is thickened, nothing is tinted, no second accent appears.

Why the counter is a second line “Applying to 40 pages…” is the job and does not change; “Page 12 of 40” is the position and changes every second or so. Splitting them keeps the sentence still while the number counts, and the figures are tabular so the line does not shuffle.

VoiceOver the screen is announced on entry: “Applying to 40 pages. Keep the app open.” The counter updates as a progress announcement, at most once a page. There are no controls, and the platform’s back gesture is refused for the seconds it runs.

S32

Applying to all pages, some pages skipped

Pages 4, 9 and 18 were not changed: their photos are gone.
Make PDF

S32 — spec

Built from ErrorLine on the pages screen the user lands back on — not on the takeover, which is gone by then. One plain sentence naming the pages that could not be rewritten because their photos were deleted.

Why naming them matters a page whose photo is gone cannot be adjusted at all, and after an apply-to-all there is otherwise no way to tell which pages changed. The sentence is the only record, so it names numbers rather than counting them.

Also silent adjusting deletes the existing PDF, because the PDF is derived from the pages. There is no dialog for that and no line about it; the footer simply reads “Make PDF” again.

VoiceOver announced once as an alert on arrival, then read above the page. The sentence is a placeholder — the engine’s wording is not in the copy tables.
